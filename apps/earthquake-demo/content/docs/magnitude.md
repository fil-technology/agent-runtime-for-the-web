# Magnitude, intensity and depth

**Magnitude** describes the energy released at the source. It is a single
number per earthquake. Each whole step up is about 32 times more energy.

**Intensity** describes shaking at a particular place, and differs from town to
town for the same earthquake. Tremor reports intensity on the Modified Mercalli
scale, from I (not felt) to XII (total destruction).

## Why a distant earthquake can be felt so far away

Two things spread shaking over a wide area:

- **Depth.** Deep earthquakes send energy into a much larger area of the
  surface than shallow ones. A deep magnitude 6 may be felt hundreds of
  kilometres away while causing little damage directly above.
- **Geology.** Soft sedimentary basins amplify and lengthen shaking, so a city
  built on a river basin can feel an earthquake far more strongly than harder
  ground the same distance away.

Subduction zone earthquakes rupture a large fault area and radiate low
frequency energy efficiently, which is why they are felt over the widest areas.

## Depth categories

Shallow earthquakes are less than 70 km deep, intermediate 70–300 km, and deep
more than 300 km. Shallow events cause the strongest shaking near the epicentre.
